1. The Four Fiduciary Duties of AI

Duty of Anti-Obfuscation (Phase 1):
The mandate that AI systems refuse to accept legacy or generic labels (such as "traffic delay" or "generic wear and tear"). If data is insufficient for a root-cause classification, the AI must trigger a hard escalation rather than guessing to satisfy metrics.
Duty of Completeness (Phase 2):
The obligation to safeguard the Unified Data Layer by correlating mechanical failure telemetry with autonomous decision history in the exact moments prior. If sensor streams are desynced, the dataset must be flagged as untrustworthy.
Duty of Diagnostic Precision (Phase 3):
The mandate that multimodal AI distinguish physical reality from sensor artifacts (e.g., shadows, road spray, or weather reflections). If the autonomous vehicle's software misclassifies an object, the diagnostic AI acts as an objective whistleblower.
Duty of Humility (Phase 4):
The architectural requirement that AI defer to human expertise whenever confidence thresholds are breached, ensuring continuous improvement through Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) feedback without self-fulfilling algorithmic drift.

3. Financial, SCOR & Telemetry Formulas

Risk-Adjusted Unit Economics:

Cost per delivery = (CapEx + OpEx + Intervention Cost + Failure Recovery Cost)

Uncovers the hidden human safety net (remote operators, SLA penalty buffers, and exception handling) to calculate genuine unit profitability.

SCOR Delivery Service Process Mapping (Orbit-Logistics):

The standard ASCM Supply Chain Operations Reference (SCOR) model is tailored specifically for fleet and delivery service operations across six core stages:

  • Plan (sP): Forecasting delivery volumes, optimizing courier/AV routes, dynamic charging schedules, and planning fleet capacity.
  • Source (sS): Procuring delivery vans/smart trucks, tracking hardware/sensors, charging energy/fuel, and managing third-party driver/teleoperator contracts.
  • Make (sM): Sorting packages at distribution hubs, automated parcel conveyance, cross-dock loading, and balancing vehicle cargo space.
  • Deliver (sD): Last-mile autonomous transportation, dynamic road pathfinding, and physical drop-off to the end customer with cryptographic proof-of-delivery (PoD).
  • Return (sR): Managing failed deliveries, automated redelivery attempts, e-commerce reverse logistics, and upstream warranty defect traceback for hardware/battery failures.
  • Enable (sE): Running cloud/edge tracking software, managing driver/AV safety compliance (EU AI Act), analyzing telemetry streams, and calculating per-mile unit economics.

Key Delivery Service Metrics Under SCOR:

SCOR Attribute Metric Category Delivery Service Metric Metric Code Definition & Business Target
Reliability Customer-Facing Perfect Shipment Delivery RL.1.1 Undamaged parcel, correct recipient, on-time SLA, verified cryptographic PoD (Target: ≥99.8%).
Responsiveness Customer-Facing Order Fulfillment Cycle Time RS.1.1 Total duration (hours/minutes) from pickup request to final drop-off (Target: ≤1.5 hrs).
Agility Customer-Facing Upside Delivery Flexibility AG.1.1 Fleet capability to absorb a 20% spike in peak/holiday volume without SLA degradation.
Cost Internal-Facing Cost per Stop / Cost per Mile CO.1.1 Total loaded operational expenses per stop and transit mile (fuel, power, wages, maintenance, compute).
Asset Management Internal-Facing Fleet Utilization AM.1.1 Percentage of vehicle cargo space utilized daily and overall active fleet uptime (≥88% target).

4. Selected Bibliography & Key Architectural References

The architectural and governance models across this book synthesize seminal methodologies in Domain-Driven Design, Enterprise Architecture frameworks, socio-technical team systems, and industrial engineering standards:

Domain-Driven Design & Strategic Modeling

  • Eric Evans — Domain-Driven Design: Tackling Complexity in the Heart of Software (Addison-Wesley, 2003). The foundational work introducing Bounded Contexts, Context Mapping patterns, Ubiquitous Language, and Strategic/Tactical DDD.
  • Vaughn Vernon — Implementing Domain-Driven Design (Addison-Wesley, 2013) & Domain-Driven Design Distilled (2016). Practical implementation blueprints for Context Mapping relationships (Shared Kernel, Customer–Supplier, Conformist, Anti-Corruption Layer, Open Host Service, Published Language) and Aggregate design.
  • Alberto Brandolini — Introducing EventStorming: An agile methodology for rapid domain exploration (Leanpub). The seminal workshop methodology exploring domain timelines via Domain Events, Commands, Aggregates, Policies, Read Models, and Hotspots.
  • Nick Tune — Architecture Modernization: Socio-technical alignment of software, structure and strategy (Manning, 2024). Pioneered Domain Message Flow Modeling, the Bounded Context Canvas, and discovery-to-context-mapping workshop recipes.
  • Mathias Verraes — Context Mapping as a Socio-Technical Discipline (verraes.net). Key heuristics on Conway's Law, power dynamics, and organizational boundaries in distributed architectures.

Enterprise Architecture & Industrial Standards

  • The Open Group — The TOGAF® Standard, 10th Edition (2022). Comprehensive standard for the Architecture Development Method (ADM Phases Preliminary through H), Enterprise Architecture Board (EAB) governance, and Architecture Change Management (Phase H / RM).
  • Automotive Industry Action Group (AIAG) — Advanced Product Quality Planning (APQP) & Production Part Approval Process (PPAP) Manuals (4th Edition). Industrial quality gate and supplier validation standards.
  • Association for Supply Chain Management (ASCM) — Supply Chain Operations Reference (SCOR) Digital Standard. Value stream mapping from raw material extraction to fleet delivery.
  • ISO/SAE 21434 & ISO 26262 — Road Vehicles: Cybersecurity Engineering & Functional Safety (ASIL-D). Deterministic automotive safety and cryptographic hardware security module (HSM) standards.

Socio-Technical Systems & Architecture Visualization

  • Matthew Skelton & Manuel Pais — Team Topologies: Organizing Business and Technology Teams for Fast Flow (IT Revolution, 2019). Team interaction modes (Collaboration, X-as-a-Service, Facilitating, Complicated Subsystem) aligned directly with DDD Context Maps.
  • Simon Brown — Software Architecture for Developers (The C4 Model). Hierarchical system context and container abstractions inspiring modern message flow notation.
