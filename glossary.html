1. The Four Fiduciary Duties of AI

Duty of Anti-Obfuscation (Phase 1):
The mandate that AI systems refuse to accept legacy or generic labels (such as "traffic delay" or "generic wear and tear"). If data is insufficient for a root-cause classification, the AI must trigger a hard escalation rather than guessing to satisfy metrics.
Duty of Completeness (Phase 2):
The obligation to safeguard the Unified Data Layer by correlating mechanical failure telemetry with autonomous decision history in the exact moments prior. If sensor streams are desynced, the dataset must be flagged as untrustworthy.
Duty of Diagnostic Precision (Phase 3):
The mandate that multimodal AI distinguish physical reality from sensor artifacts (e.g., shadows, road spray, or weather reflections). If the autonomous vehicle's software misclassifies an object, the diagnostic AI acts as an objective whistleblower.
Duty of Humility (Phase 4):
The architectural requirement that AI defer to human expertise whenever confidence thresholds are breached, ensuring continuous improvement through Human-in-the-Loop (HITL) feedback without self-fulfilling algorithmic drift.

3. Financial, SCOR & Telemetry Formulas

Risk-Adjusted Unit Economics:

Cost per delivery = (CapEx + OpEx + Intervention Cost + Failure Recovery Cost)

Uncovers the hidden human safety net (remote operators, SLA penalty buffers, and exception handling) to calculate genuine unit profitability.

SCOR Delivery Service Process Mapping (Orbit-Logistics):

The standard ASCM Supply Chain Operations Reference (SCOR) model is tailored specifically for fleet and delivery service operations across six core stages:

  • Plan (sP): Forecasting delivery volumes, optimizing courier/AV routes, dynamic charging schedules, and planning fleet capacity.
  • Source (sS): Procuring delivery vans/smart trucks, tracking hardware/sensors, charging energy/fuel, and managing third-party driver/teleoperator contracts.
  • Make (sM): Sorting packages at distribution hubs, automated parcel conveyance, cross-dock loading, and balancing vehicle cargo space.
  • Deliver (sD): Last-mile autonomous transportation, dynamic road pathfinding, and physical drop-off to the end customer with cryptographic proof-of-delivery (PoD).
  • Return (sR): Managing failed deliveries, automated redelivery attempts, e-commerce reverse logistics, and upstream warranty defect traceback for hardware/battery failures.
  • Enable (sE): Running cloud/edge tracking software, managing driver/AV safety compliance (EU AI Act), analyzing telemetry streams, and calculating per-mile unit economics.

Core Architecture, Regulatory & Governance Terms:

Architecture Contract (TOGAF® Phase F & G):
An enterprise architecture governance instrument defined under the TOGAF® Standard. It represents a joint accountability agreement between the Architecture Board, business sponsors, and delivery teams that establishes architecture deliverables, non-functional quality envelopes, and conformance metrics. While distinct from external commercial contracts, in multi-enterprise consortia it is formally mirrored in vendor Master Services Agreements (MSAs) and inter-company SLAs.
TIME Framework (Gartner, Inc.):
An industry application portfolio rationalization framework developed by Gartner (Tolerate, Invest, Migrate, Eliminate). Categorizes software systems by technical health and strategic business value to prioritize modernization, refactoring, or retirement during TOGAF Phase C (Information Systems Architectures).
EU AI Act Conformity Pathways (Regulation (EU) 2024/1689):
The European regulatory compliance framework for high-risk artificial intelligence. Distinguishes standalone logistics and fleet dispatch systems (Annex III Point 2, governed by provider Internal Control under Annex VI, Article 43(2) with ISO/IEC 42001 certification) from vehicle guidance and braking safety systems (governed under Article 6(1) and Annex I Section B via the automotive EU Type-Approval Framework under Regulation (EU) 2018/858 / GSR 2019/2144). Statutory penalties are structured under Article 99(4) (up to €15,000,000 or 3% of worldwide annual turnover for high-risk non-compliance) versus Article 99(3) (€35,000,000 or 7% for prohibited practices under Article 5).
Shapley Additive exPlanations (SHAP):
A cooperative game-theoretic method for explaining individual machine learning predictions by computing Shapley feature attributions \(\phi_i\) that satisfy local accuracy (\(\sum \phi_i = f(x) - \mathbb{E}[f(x)]\)). In high-integrity operations, explainability is quantified using local surrogate fidelity (\(R^2 \ge 0.95\)) and top-\(k\) attribution coverage (\(\ge 95\%\) of prediction variance explained) rather than a naked percentage probability.
Model Distribution Drift (\(D_{\text{KS}}\) & \(\text{PSI}\)):
Statistical variance between baseline training distributions and production inference telemetry. Quantified using rigorous statistical metrics, including the Kolmogorov-Smirnov test statistic \(D_{\text{KS}} \ge 0.08\) and Population Stability Index \(\text{PSI} \ge 0.10\), triggering automated safe fallbacks and model retraining loops.
